The Mars Sample Mission

One launch. One rover. One chance to find evidence of ancient life in Jezero Crater. Design a rover that can survive entry, land safely, drill and analyze samples, stay warm through dust storms, and send its discoveries back to Earth — without exceeding the launch mass or mission budget.

Your single, complex objective

Deliver a science rover to Jezero Crater and complete a two-year search for ancient microbial biosignatures. Your design must pass every engineering constraint before launch. The choices are connected: more instruments cost more and add mass; extra fuel improves arrival margin but also consumes payload capacity; solar power is lighter, but dust storms and winter darkness threaten the mission.
